DoorRepo - the palette, and what each colour currently means
=============================================================================

Eight colours, foreground and background, plus a bold flag. That is the
whole range. On most terminals bold brightens the foreground, so the
practical palette is sixteen foregrounds over eight backgrounds. A real
Amiga running a real terminal is the constraint here, not the browser.

  0  black      4  blue
  1  red        5  magenta
  2  green      6  cyan
  3  yellow     7  white

How the door uses them today
----------------------------

Counted from the source, most-used first, so you can see the actual weight
of each rather than a designer's intention:

  white on black            body text everywhere - list rows, DIZ, docs,
                            file listings, history
  yellow on black, bold     anything the user must notice: an empty state,
                            a refusal, "nothing to show here"
  white on blue, bold       the header bar
  cyan on black             panel borders and their labels
  yellow on blue, bold      the header's highlighted field
  green on black, bold      success, and an enabled door
  cyan on black, bold       footer key legend
  red on black, bold        failures, and a junk/ad file in a listing

Two rules behind that, worth keeping whatever the design becomes:

  RED MEANS SOMETHING WENT WRONG OR IS UNWANTED. It is used for failed
  actions in the history and for advertising files inside an archive.
  Nothing decorative is red.

  GREEN MEANS THE THING IS LIVE. An enabled door, a completed action.

What is safe to change
----------------------

Everything else. The border colour, the header treatment, whether panels
have shadows, whether the selected row is reverse-video or a coloured bar,
the footer's look, the box characters. None of that is load-bearing.

What is NOT yours to colour
---------------------------

The FILE_ID.DIZ shown in the detail pane is another artist's work, taken
from the archive as it was authored. The door prints it verbatim - its own
colours, its own line breaks, clipped but never re-wrapped and never
recoloured. Design AROUND it, not over it.

The same goes for documentation and AmigaGuide text.

One caution about backgrounds
-----------------------------

A filled background over the whole screen costs bytes on every repaint,
and a good number of these users are on a modem. The door repaints the
list and the detail pane on every keypress. Large flat fills are fine;
per-cell colour changes across a full-width region are what get
expensive - each one is an escape sequence.
